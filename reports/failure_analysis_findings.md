# Failure analysis — findings

Interpretation of [`failure_analysis.md`](failure_analysis.md) (tables generated by
`scripts/failure_analysis.py`) after a manual review of the samples that most models got wrong.
All numbers are from the v0.1 test set (100 samples, 6 models, zero-shot).

**Main conclusion:** a large share of the measured errors are caused by the evaluation itself
(gold labels, entity value matching and one prompt definition), not by models misunderstanding
Myanglish. These need fixing before the scores can justify (or rule out) fine-tuning.

## 1. Which business intents are hardest?

| Hardest (mean F1) | Why |
|---|---|
| `unknown` (70.0) | Models give vague messages a concrete intent. 5 of 6 models read `yout pyi lar` ("Has it arrived yet?", `mbb_0024`) as `delivery_inquiry`; arguably the gold label is the debatable one. |
| `greeting` (79.0) | Missed 18 times, 12 of them with nothing else wrong. **This is caused by our prompt**, which defines greeting as "hello / hi ... *with no other request*", while 3 of the 10 gold greetings are combined with another intent (`mbb_0073`, `mbb_0075`, `mbb_0089`). Models followed the prompt. |
| `product_inquiry` (80.3) | Both missed (20) and over-predicted (12). The boundary with `stock_availability` and `price_inquiry` is not clear in the labels (see section 5). |
| `price_inquiry` (82.9) | Mostly missed when combined with discount questions (`2 khu yuu yin shrot pay lar` = "will you reduce the price if I buy two?"). All 6 models labelled `mbb_0059` as discount only. |

Easiest: `refund_exchange` (95.4), `discount_inquiry` (92.9), `payment_inquiry` (92.7). These
messages tend to contain English keywords (refund, discount, KPay).

## 2. Does complexity cause errors?

Yes, but in a specific way: **models under-label multi-intent messages**.

- Exact match drops from 72–97% on single-intent messages to 48–74% on two-intent messages,
  for every model.
- Of the wrong answers on two-intent messages, 53 had *fewer* intents than gold and only 5 had
  more. Models usually find the main intent and drop the secondary one.
- Three-intent messages scored *higher* than two-intent ones for most models (only 8 samples,
  so this is noise more than signal).
- Part of the drop comes from gold labels, not models (section 5): several two-intent gold
  labels add a second intent that is hard to justify from the text.

## 3. Are models missing or hallucinating entities?

Precision (55–67%) is well below recall (67–88%) for every model, so on paper the main problem is
**extra entities**, not missed ones. Manual review shows most "extras" are not hallucinations:

| Cause of "extra" entities | Example | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Gold label missing | `mbb_0055` "kbz pay lo pay loh ya ma lr cb pay lo ya lar" has no gold entities; every model correctly extracts KBZPay and CB Pay | Evaluation |
| Free-text values never match | `issue` gold "tracking not provided" vs model "tracking number not sent" (112 of the extras are `issue`) | Evaluation |
| Value format differences | gold "Yankin, Yangon" (one value) vs models "yankin" + "yangon"; gold "sneakers" vs "sneaker" | Evaluation |
| Over-extraction | `date_time` "now" from *ahku* ("now"), `variant` "original" | Model (minor) |

Missed entities are the real model weakness for Gemma (recall 67%) and DeepSeek (75%).

## 4. Which information is hardest to extract?

| Entity | Result | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| `color`, `quantity`, `size` | 88–100 F1 for every model | Easy: short, often English or digits |
| `issue` | 0–25 F1 | Free-text paraphrase; exact matching cannot score it |
| `currency` | 0 F1 for 5 models | Gold adds `MMK` for prices written as "10 thaung" (100,000); models do not infer an unstated currency |
| `date_time` | 53–73 F1, precision 48–71 | Models extract more time expressions than are labelled |
| `product`, `variant`, `price` | very model-dependent | Few gold examples (4–7), so single errors swing F1 by 15+ points |

## 5. Are errors linguistic or evaluation-related?

Review of the 16 samples that 3 or more models got wrong (intent set) plus the entity errors above:

| Category | Samples | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Debatable gold label** | ~8 | `mbb_0059`, `mbb_0067`, `mbb_0053` (gold adds `price_inquiry`/`product_inquiry` that all models leave out), `mbb_0024`, `mbb_0069` |
| **Prompt definition conflict** | 3 | `mbb_0073`, `mbb_0075`, `mbb_0089` (greeting + another intent) |
| **Genuinely ambiguous** | 3 | `mbb_0094` (cancel blue + keep red: is that also `place_order`?), `mbb_0099`, `mbb_0061` |
| **Linguistic** | 2–3 | `mbb_0051` "ph ph tr lay tr ya ml lar" (heavy abbreviation; 4 models said `unknown`); `mbb_0088` "ah phone ka kwal" — *phone* here is the Burmese word for "lid" (အဖုံး), and every model read it as an English "phone" and reported "phone cracked" |

Also notable: **9 of the 16 hard samples were written by DeepSeek**, whose samples carry the most
multi-intent labels. The difficulty of a sample partly reflects which model wrote and labelled it.

Conclusion: roughly **3 in 4 of the hardest failures are evaluation-related**. True linguistic
failures exist (`mbb_0088` is a good example of Myanglish/English false friends) but are a minority.

## 6. Which models suit real-time support?

Using a p90 budget of 3 seconds per reply:

| Model | Median | p90 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma | 0.9 s | 2.2 s | Real-time ready, cheapest, but weakest accuracy |
| GPT | 2.1 s | 3.3 s | Borderline; best accuracy/latency balance |
| Claude | 2.4 s | 3.4 s | Borderline |
| Gemini | 5.2 s | 8.2 s | Too slow for live chat (one request took 132 s) |
| Qwen | 6.9 s | 14.2 s | Too slow |
| DeepSeek | 15.1 s | 40.3 s | Too slow; generates ~900 reasoning tokens per message |

Latency was measured during the benchmark run with parallel requests, so treat it as indicative.
Reasoning-heavy models (DeepSeek, Qwen) are slow because they generate many more output tokens,
not because of network time.

## Recommended next steps (before any fine-tuning)

1. **Fix the evaluation, as v0.2** (v0.1 stays frozen): re-check the gold labels of the samples
   in section 5, add missing entities (e.g. `mbb_0055`), and agree whether a secondary intent
   must be explicit in the text.
2. **Fix the prompt**: allow `greeting` together with other intents, then rerun (about $2).
3. **Score entities fairly**: either constrain `issue` to a fixed list of issue types or score it
   with a lenient/semantic match; treat multi-part locations consistently.
4. **Then** run few-shot evaluation and collect real customer messages; only if models still fail
   on real Myanglish (false friends like *phone* = lid, heavy abbreviations) is fine-tuning justified.
